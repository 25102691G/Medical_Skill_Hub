# 成人短肠综合征诊疗中国专家共识(2026版)重要信息索引

## Definitions

- **Adult SBS**: remnant functional small bowel ≤200 cm, leading to diarrhea, fluid/electrolyte disorders, and malabsorption of nutrients.
- **IF**: inability to absorb sufficient water, macronutrients, micronutrients, and electrolytes, requiring intravenous supplementation (IVS) to maintain health.
- **SBS‑IF**: concurrent SBS and IF.
- **Functional SBS**: remnant >200 cm yet SBS symptoms due to extensive mucosal disease; manage as SBS.

## Diagnostic Criteria and Classification

- **Recommendation 1** (C, Strong): Intraoperative direct measurement is the gold standard for remnant length; if not performed, use barium contrast, CT, or MRI enterography for indirect estimation.
- **Recommendation 2** (C, Strong): Three anatomical types (Figure 1):
  - **Type I (jejunostomy)**: only small bowel, no colon connection.
  - **Type II (jejuno‑colonic anastomosis)**: jejunum anastomosed to colon.
  - **Type III (jejuno‑ileal anastomosis)**: jejuno‑ileal anastomosis with intact ileocecal valve and colon.
  - Spontaneous PN‑independence probabilities: ≈20%, 40%, 80% respectively.
- **Recommendation 3** (B, Strong): Natural history in three phases:
  - Acute (<4 weeks): massive fluid/electrolyte loss, gastric hypersecretion; focus on stabilization.
  - Adaptation (1–2 years): bowel hypertrophy, functional adaptation; promote enteral autonomy.
  - Maintenance (long‑term): achieve nutritional balance or chronic PN dependence.
- **Recommendation 4** (B, Strong): Severity classified by the simplified 8‑class clinical intestinal failure (CIF) system (Table 3). Based on daily IV volume (≤1000, 1001‑2000, 2001‑3000, >3000 mL) and type (A: fluid/electrolyte only, B: PN).

## Treatment

### Treatment Goals (R5, C, Strong)

- Provide adequate nutrition, promote intestinal adaptation, restore intestinal autonomy, and prevent complications.

### Multidisciplinary Team (R6, C, Strong)

- Core members: GI surgeon, gastroenterologist, nurse, dietitian, clinical pharmacist, social worker, psychologist. MDT improves safety, reduces PN/EN complications, and enhances quality of life.

### Nutrition and Dietary Therapy

- **R7** (A, Strong): Early PN, transition to EN/oral feeding as tolerated.
  - Type I often needs lifelong PN; Type II may require supplemental PN; Type III often weans off PN.
- **R8** (A, Strong): For stable patients unable to meet needs orally/enterally, start HPN.
- **R9** (B, Strong): HPN: full‑nutrient formula, total energy 83.68–146.44 kJ/kg/d (20–35 kcal/kg/d), adjusted individually.
- **R10** (A, Strong): Patient/caregiver trained by NST before HPN; ongoing NST monitoring mandatory.
- **R11** (B, Weak): Central venous access for HPN (tunneled or implanted port, PICC for short‑term), managed by NST.
- **R12** (B, Strong): Aseptic PN preparation; flush with 20 mL saline after infusion; lock catheter. No routine antibiotic lock (2% taurolidine if long‑term).
- **R13** (B, Strong): Maximize oral/EN intake. Avoid prolonged high‑calorie/high‑glucose PN. For PN >6 months, use non‑soy lipid emulsions (olive/fish oil or MCT/LCT) to prevent IFALD. Glucose 3–6 g/kg/d, blood glucose <10 mmol/L.
- **R14** (B, Strong): Full micronutrient assessment initially; monitor periodically; supplement deficiencies preferably orally/enterally. Monitor bone metabolism and density every 1–2 years.
- **R15** (C, Strong): In adaptation phase, for low PN dependence, tube feeding EN (continuous then intermittent) plus oral intake. Start isotonic polymeric formula; elemental if intolerant. Monitor GI tolerance (cut‑off: output ≥2000 mL/d or 40 mL/kg/d).
- **R16** (B, Strong): For clinical dehydration/sodium depletion (urine <800 mL/d, Na <35 mmol/d): drink ORS; avoid hypotonic fluids.
- **R17** (B, Strong): Non‑acute: regular foods, frequent meals, nutrition counseling; ONS if malnutrition risk.
- **R18** (C, Strong): Low mono‑/disaccharides. Colon‑preserved: high complex carbohydrates, low fat, high MCT; monitor essential fatty acids and fat‑soluble vitamins. No colon: no fat/carbohydrate restriction.
- **R19** (C, Weak): Lactose only restricted if intolerant (<20 g/d). Insufficient evidence for soluble fiber, glutamine, probiotics.

### General Pharmacotherapy

- **R20** (B, Weak): Prefer non‑oral routes. TDM for narrow therapeutic index drugs or poor response.
- **R21** (B, Strong): Post‑resection hypergastrinemia/high output: acid suppressors (PPI preferred IV). Long‑term: monitor SIBO, vitamin B₁₂, Mg, Ca, fractures.
- **R22** (B, Strong): Somatostatin/octreotide for high‑output jejunostomy; octreotide may increase gallstone risk.
- **R23** (B, Strong): Antimotility agents: loperamide 6–16 mg/d before meals and bedtime (higher if no ileum). Contraindicated in infectious enteritis; ECG for high doses.
- **R24** (B, Weak): Bile acid diarrhea (ileal resection <100 cm): cholestyramine 8 g/d or colesevelam 2.5 g/d, taken apart from other drugs.
- **R25** (B, Strong): Fat malabsorption: pancreatic enzyme replacement, start 40,000 USP lipase per meal.
- **R26** (B, Strong): SIBO: rifaximin for 10–14 days; rotate antibiotics if no response.
- **R27** (C, Weak): After SIBO control, consider specific probiotics (e.g., VSL#3) to lower oxalate absorption.

### Non‑Transplant Surgery

- **R28** (C, Strong): Initial surgery: maximal bowel preservation, minimal resection; preserve ileocecal valve and colon.
- **R29** (B, Strong): Restore continuity when stable; first assess distal bowel health.
- **R30** (B, Weak): For persistent PN dependence (>6 mo) with dilated bowel (>4 cm), consider autologous intestinal reconstruction (AIRS): STEP, LILT, or SILT. STEP: 60–80% lengthening, 36% PN wean rate. Reverse segment for rapid transit.

### Intestinal Growth Factors

- **R31** (A, Strong): Teduglutide (GLP‑2 analogue) 0.05 mg/kg/d SC. Response (≥20% PS volume reduction): 64% at 6 m, 77% at 1 y, 82% at ≥2 y; complete weaning: 11%, 17%, 21% respectively.
- **R32** (A, Strong): Evaluate response at 6 months, then every 6 months; discontinue if no improvement at 12 months.
- **R33** (C, Weak): Short‑term GH ± glutamine for non‑responders; effects transient.

## Complications

### Hepatobiliary

- **R35** (C, Strong): Monitor for IFALD; early oral/EN and restoration of continuity reduce biliary stasis. Ursodeoxycholic acid and fish‑oil lipids may help.
- **R36** (C, Weak): Cholecystectomy not routinely prophylactic; consider only for symptomatic stones.

### Renal and Metabolic

- **R37** (C, Strong): Renal injury from chronic dehydration/high output; manage fluid loss, adjust PN in CKD.
- **R38** (C, Strong): Urinary stones: calcium oxalate stones (low‑fat diet, oral calcium, bile acid binders, potassium citrate); uric acid stones (fluids, potassium citrate, urine pH 6.0–6.5, allopurinol if needed).
- **R39** (C, Strong): Metabolic bone disease (MBD): correct Mg/Ca deficiency, avoid long‑term PPI, treat acidosis.
- **R40** (C, Strong): MBD treatment: calcium 1200–1500 mg/d, vitamin D 200 IU/d IV or 20,000–50,000 IU/week orally, bisphosphonates or teriparatide/denosumab.

## Follow‑up and Patient Education

- **R41** (C, Strong): Structured follow‑up every 3–12 months (Table 4). Monitor weight/BMI/grip strength, prealbumin (>180 mg/L), fecal fat (<7 g/d ideal), electrolytes, micronutrients, bone density, gallbladder ultrasound.
- **R42** (C, Strong): Encourage participation in patient organisations for support and advocacy.
